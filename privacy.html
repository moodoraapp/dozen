Dozen

Privacy Policy

Effective November 21, 2026

Dozen (“we”, “us”) is a meal planning app. This policy explains what information Dozen uses, why, and the choices you have. In short: your plan is built on your phone, there’s no account to create, we collect only what we need to run the app, we don’t sell your information, and we don’t show ads.

Information you give us

To plan your week, Dozen asks for your household size, how many dinners you want, your weekly grocery budget, your ZIP code and store, your diet and foods you won’t eat, how long you want to cook, and the items you always have at home. Dozen also keeps your weekly plan, favorites, grocery list check marks, the items and prices you add to your list, recipes you save from websites, your reminder time and text size.

This information is stored on your phone. If iCloud is on, Apple also keeps a copy in your iCloud account, so your plan comes back if you reinstall Dozen and stays the same on your other devices signed in to the same Apple Account. This copy goes to Apple, not to us; we can’t see it. We use your ZIP code only to estimate your region’s grocery prices; Dozen doesn’t ask for or use your device’s location.

No account

Dozen doesn’t have accounts. You don’t sign in, and we don’t ask for your name or email address.

Recipe and price reports

When you report a problem with a recipe or a price, we receive the recipe or item, your answer, the price and pack size you entered, any note you write, your store and region, the time of the report and the app version. Reports don’t include anything that identifies you or your device, and we use them to correct recipes and prices. Google, which stores reports for us, receives your device’s IP address when a report is sent, as with any internet request; we don’t store it with the report.

Subscriptions

Apple handles payment; we never see your card details. We use RevenueCat to check whether your subscription or free trial is active. RevenueCat receives your purchase history and subscription status with a random identifier that Dozen creates on your phone; it isn’t linked to your name or email. On a new phone, tap Restore on the subscription screen to unlock Premium with the same Apple Account.

How the app is used

We use PostHog to understand which features people use and where they get stuck, for example whether the welcome steps were completed, a free trial was started or a recipe was opened. PostHog receives the screens you open and a few actions (starting or restoring a purchase, planning a new week, sending a report), with the app version, device model, iOS version, language and time zone, and a random identifier that PostHog creates on your phone. It isn’t linked to your name or email, and your answers, ZIP code and plan aren’t sent. We set PostHog not to store your IP address or look up your location from it, and we don’t use this information for advertising.

Apple Reminders

If you add your grocery list to Apple’s Reminders app, Dozen asks for access to Reminders. It shows the names of your lists so you can pick one, writes your grocery items to the list you choose, keeps them up to date when your plan changes and reads which of them are checked off. This happens on your phone and in your iCloud account; nothing from Reminders is sent to us.

Recipes from websites

If you add a recipe from a link, Dozen opens that web page on your phone to read the recipe, so the website receives the request as with any visit. The recipe is saved on your phone and in your iCloud account, not on our servers.

Notifications

Dinner and free-trial reminders are scheduled on your phone. Dozen doesn’t use push notifications, so we don’t collect a notification token.

What we don’t do

  • We don’t sell or rent your personal information, and we don’t share it for cross-context behavioral advertising.
  • We don’t show ads and don’t track you across other companies’ apps or websites.
  • We don’t access your contacts, photos, camera, microphone or location.

Service providers

We use these companies to run Dozen. They process information only on our behalf and under their own security commitments:

  • Google Firebase (Cloud Firestore): storage of recipe and price reports, in the United States.
  • RevenueCat: subscription status.
  • PostHog: product analytics, stored in the European Union.
  • Apple: iCloud sync of your plan and App Store payments.

How long we keep information

We don’t keep your plan on our servers. It stays on your phone and in your iCloud account until you erase it. Reports don’t identify you and may be kept to maintain price and recipe accuracy. RevenueCat keeps purchase records for as long as they’re needed to provide your subscription and for accounting. Analytics events are kept for up to 12 months.

Your choices and rights

  • Erase your plan, list, favorites, pantry and answers from your phone and from iCloud in the app: Profile › Erase all data. If you use Dozen on more than one device, do this on each of them.
  • Change or remove your answers at any time in Profile.
  • Turn reminders off in Profile or in iPhone Settings.
  • Ask us for a copy of your information, or to correct or delete it, by emailing moodora.app@gmail.com.

Depending on where you live, for example in California, you may have additional rights to know, access, correct and delete your personal information, and to not be discriminated against for using them. We honor these requests for all users. We may need to confirm your identity before acting on a request.

Children

Dozen isn’t directed to children under 13, and we don’t knowingly collect personal information from them. If you believe a child has given us information, contact us and we will delete it.

Security

Information is encrypted in transit, and access to our systems is limited to what is needed to run Dozen. No method of storage or transmission is completely secure, but we work to protect your information.

Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we will update the date above and, for significant changes, tell you in the app before they take effect.

Contact
